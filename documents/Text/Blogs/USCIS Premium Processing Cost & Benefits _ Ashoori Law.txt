Decoding Premium Processing
In a nutshell, USCIS premium processing is an optional service provided by USCIS for certain types of cases. It allows applicants to expedite the processing of their applications by paying an additional fee. The USCIS premium processing service is particularly useful for those who seek quicker responses from USCIS. For instance, if the standard processing time for an
L1 Visa
petition is six months, utilizing premium processing involves paying an extra fee, currently set at $2,500, to have the case reviewed within just 15 days.
A New Angle on Premium Processing
While the expedited processing offered by USCIS premium processing can be a game-changer, we are here to present an alternative viewpoint that is often overlooked when considering expedited USCIS processing. Many applicants are drawn to USCIS premium processing due to its accelerated timeline, but there’s more to consider before making a decision.
The Time Factor and Its Implications
When an applicant opts for the USCIS premium processing service, USCIS is legally obligated to issue a response within the designated time frame, usually 15 days for most eligible applications under USCIS Premium Processing. However, this doesn’t necessarily mean that a final decision will be made within this window. USCIS may either approve the case or issue a Request for Evidence (RFE), seeking additional information or clarification.
The Potential for Request for Evidence
Consider this scenario: you submit an extensive application under USCIS premium processing, potentially spanning hundreds of pages, for premium processing. The immigration officer assigned to your case also has a 15-day deadline to review and respond. Given the substantial amount of material to evaluate within a limited time, there is a possibility, albeit unofficial, that the chances of receiving an RFE could increase.
Understanding the Unspoken Dynamics
While there are no official statistics indicating a direct link between the premium processing service and a higher likelihood of receiving an RFE, discussions within the immigration community have pointed to a potential correlation. As a seasoned immigration lawyer, I’ve observed instances where cases under USCIS premium processing seemingly encountered a more pronounced likelihood of RFE issuance due to the time constraints placed on immigration officers.
The Refund Quandary
USCIS’s premium processing commitment isn’t just about expediting processing; it’s also about providing a response within the stipulated time. If the response isn’t issued within the designated window, USCIS is obligated to refund the premium processing fee. Naturally, USCIS aims to avoid this refund scenario.
